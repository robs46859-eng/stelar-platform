1. Ideal Customer Profile
"Prepared Paige"
A 29–36-year-old, college-educated, household-income $90K–$200K woman in her first pregnancy or first year postpartum. She's a researcher by temperament: she Googles every twinge, has 4 pregnancy apps on her phone, and has already bought 2–3 books. She values feeling prepared over being frugal. She lives on Instagram and her group chat. She buys digital products impulsively between 9pm and 1am while breastfeeding or unable to sleep.

Demographics
Age	29–36 (skews 30–34)
Household income	$90K–$200K; often dual-income or partner is higher earner
Education	Bachelor's or higher (~70%)
Geography	US metros + suburbs of metros; coastal + Texas/Colorado overindexed
Work	Knowledge worker on leave, or recently returned. Some quit / went part-time.
Marital status	~85% partnered/married
Primary device	iPhone. Phone-first, not desktop.
Psychographics
High agency, high anxiety. She wants to do this right. Failure feels catastrophic. → Sells: certainty, expert validation, checklists.
Time-poor, attention-fragmented. Postpartum she has 7-minute windows. → Sells: short, immediately usable, no "module 1 of 12."
Identity in flux. She's grieving her pre-mom self while loving her baby. Marketing that pretends this isn't true falls flat.
Distrusts generic advice, trusts named experts + peers. "My IBCLC says…" / "Other moms in my Dec 2025 bumper group said…"
Will pay $30 to avoid 2 hours of Googling. Time > money in this season. This is the core arbitrage a $10–50 product exploits.
Jobs-to-be-done
Pregnancy (1st-timers):

"Help me feel ready for labor without taking a $400 course"
"Tell me what to actually put on my registry"
"What do I pack / buy / do in the 3rd trimester"
"Decode my symptoms — is this normal?"
"Help me write a birth plan my OB will respect"
Postpartum / nursing (0–12mo):

"Get my baby to sleep"
"Fix breastfeeding pain / supply / latch tonight"
"What do I eat to heal / lose weight / boost supply"
"Am I okay? Is this PPD?"
"Get my body back without hating myself"
"Schedule / routine for a 3-month-old / 6-month-old"
Watering holes (ranked by value)
Instagram — Reels, carousels. Saves > likes.
TikTok — mom-tok; broader income skew, still reaches middle/upper.
Podcasts — The Birth Hour, Pregnancy Podcast, Pregnancy & Birth Made Easy, Boob to Food.
Facebook bumper groups — e.g. "December 2025 Mamas." Highest trust, hardest access.
Reddit — r/BabyBumps, r/beyondthebump, r/breastfeeding, r/sleeptrain.
Peanut app — niche, high-intent.
Pinterest — undervalued for evergreen guides/checklists; long traffic tail.
Buying behavior
Impulse window: 9pm–1am, while feeding or rocking. Mobile checkout + Apple Pay essential.
Price ladder: Will buy $19 on a whim. $49 needs one piece of social proof. $99+ needs a sales page + testimonials.
Triggers: A specific scary moment (failed latch, can't sleep, OB appointment tomorrow). Sell to the moment, not the season.
Refund-tolerant: Generous refund policy reduces friction more than it costs.
Bundle-receptive: "Hospital bag checklist + birth plan template + week-by-week" beats any single component at $29.
Common objections
"Isn't this free on Instagram?" → Edge: organized, expert-vetted, no scrolling.
"I already bought [Taking Cara Babies / Expectful / etc.]" → Position complementary, not competing.
"I don't have time to read another thing." → Lead with format: "10-min read," "1-page PDF," "audio version included."
Messaging angles that convert
Specificity over completeness: "The 11 things to actually pack in your hospital bag (not the 47 every list tells you)."
Permission-giving: "You don't have to read 4 books to be a good mom."
Time-to-relief promise: "Get your baby latching in 20 minutes."
Named-expert credibility: Co-create with an IBCLC, RN, doula, pelvic-floor PT, or RD
