# Week 7: Days 43-49 Postpartum

## Body: Maternal Recovery & Breastfeeding Continuation

By week seven, your body continues to heal and adjust to postpartum life. Most lochia (postpartum bleeding) has likely stopped or become very light spotting. Uterine involution is nearly complete, and any perineal tears or episiotomy wounds should be well-healed. You may notice increased energy levels as sleep patterns begin to stabilize slightly, though interrupted sleep is still common.

**Breastfeeding Continuation**: At 6-7 weeks postpartum, breastfeeding is typically well-established. Your milk supply has regulated to match your baby's demand, and feeding sessions may become more efficient and predictable. Many babies develop a more regular feeding pattern, though growth spurts can still cause temporary increases in frequency. If you're pumping, you may notice changes in output as your body adjusts.

**Physical Recovery Tips**:
- Gradually reintroduce light exercise if cleared by your provider (walking, gentle yoga, pelvic floor exercises)
- Continue prioritizing protein-rich foods and hydration to support healing and milk production
- Monitor your menstrual cycle return—some women may experience their first postpartum period around this time, especially if not exclusively breastfeeding
- Practice good posture during feeding to prevent neck and back strain
- Consider scheduling your 6-week postpartum checkup if you haven't already

## Baby: Newborn Care & Feeding

Your baby is now approximately 1.5 months old and showing significant developmental progress. They are becoming more alert, interactive, and responsive to their environment.

**Feeding Patterns**: Expect 6-8 feeds per day for breastfed babies (slightly fewer than earlier weeks as stomach capacity increases). Formula-fed babies may take 4-5 oz per feeding every 3-4 hours. Watch for feeding cues rather than sticking to a strict schedule. Growth spurts around 6 weeks can temporarily increase feeding frequency.

**Developmental Milestones**:
- Begins to smile socially (not just reflexive smiles) in response to voices and faces
- Tracks objects smoothly with eyes and may reach for nearby objects
- Shows improved head control during tummy time (steadily holding head up for longer periods)
- Makes cooing and gurgling sounds beyond crying
- May begin to recognize familiar faces and voices from a distance

**Sleep Patterns**: Total sleep may range from 14-16 hours per day, with slightly longer nighttime stretches emerging (though not yet "sleeping through the night"). Daytime naps may start to consolidate into more predictable patterns.

**Introduction to Solids Prep**: While actual solid food introduction typically begins around 6 months, you can start observing readiness signs:
- Good head and neck control
- Ability to sit up with support
- Showing interest in food you're eating
- Loss of tongue-thrust reflex (pushing food out with tongue)
Note: Exclusive breastfeeding is recommended for the first 6 months; solids preparation refers to mental readiness and observing developmental cues.

## Mental Health: Navigating Ongoing Adjustment

Week seven often brings a mix of growing confidence and new adjustments. The initial "baby blues" have typically resolved, but you may experience waves of anxiety, overwhelm, or mood fluctuations as you settle into your new role. Hormonal shifts continue, and the reality of ongoing sleep deprivation can impact emotional regulation.

If feelings of sadness, hopelessness, or anxiety persist, feel severe, or interfere with daily functioning, consider discussing postpartum depression (PPD) or anxiety (PPA) with your provider. The Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale (EPDS) remains a useful screening tool; scores ≥10 warrant professional discussion.

**Coping Strategies**:
- Continue protecting small pockets of time for self-care (even 10-15 minutes for a shower, tea, or short walk)
- Maintain connections with other parents for normalization and support
- Practice mindfulness or breathing exercises during feeding sessions
- Adjust expectations—focus on "good enough" parenting rather than perfection
- Communicate openly with your partner about shifting needs and responsibilities

## Partner Playbook: How to Help in Week 7

As you establish more predictable routines, your partner's support evolves to match changing needs. Week seven is a good time to refine systems that prevent burnout and promote teamwork.

- **Feeding Support**: If breastfeeding, partner can handle burping, diaper changes, and settling baby after feeds. If pumping, they can wash pump parts and store milk. Consider alternating morning feeds to allow each parent longer sleep blocks periodically.
- **Household Balance**: Re-evaluate division of chores as energy levels shift. Partner can take lead on meal planning, grocery shopping, or specific household projects.
- **Baby Development Engagement**: Encourage partner to engage in tummy time, reading, singing, and face-to-face interaction to support baby's development and bonding.
- **Emotional Labor Sharing**: Partner can initiate check-ins about mental load—remembering appointments, tracking supplies, anticipating needs—not just task execution.
- **Self-Care Facilitation**: Actively protect time for each parent to recharge individually, whether through exercise, hobbies, or social connections.
- **Return-to-Work Planning**: If applicable, begin discussing logistics for return to work (childcare arrangements, pumping schedules, household adjustments during transitions).

## Call Doctor? Red-Flag Table for Week 7

|| Symptom | Normal | When to Call |
||---------|--------|--------------|
|| **Bleeding (Lochia)** | Very light spotting or yellowish discharge; may be intermittent. | Return to bright red bleeding, soaking more than one light pad per hour, foul odor, fever/chills. |
|| **Perineal/Abdominal Pain** | Mild occasional discomfort; healing continues. | Increasing pain, pus, red streaks, fever, or pain that interferes with daily activities. |
|| **Breast Pain** | Occasional fullness before feeds; mild tenderness possible. | Persistent nipple pain, cracked/bleeding nipples, flu-like symptoms (fever, chills), localized red/warm breast area (mastitis). |
|| **Baby's Fever** | None expected; mild fluctuations normal. | Temperature ≥100.4°F (38°C) rectal — could indicate infection requiring prompt evaluation. |
|| **Emotional Health** | Occasional waves of overwhelm or anxiety; generally coping well. | Persistent sadness/hopelessness >2 weeks, inability to enjoy baby, anxiety/panic attacks, thoughts of self-harm or harming baby, hallucinations/delusions. |
|| **Baby's Feeding/Output** | Content after feeds, 6+ wet diapers/day, steady weight gain. | Poor weight gain, projectile vomiting, bloody stools, lethargy, fewer than 6 wet diapers/day. |
|| **Baby's Development** | Smiling, tracking objects, improving head control, vocalizing. | No visual tracking, no response to sound, lack of movement in limbs, persistent crossed eyes after 3 months. |
|| **Urination/Bowel Movements** | Regular urination; bowel movements vary (daily to every few days). | Painful urination, blood in urine, constipation with pain/blood, diarrhea with dehydration signs. |

**Remember**: When in doubt, call your provider. It's always better to check.
