# Week 10: Days 64-70 Postpartum

## Body: Maternal Recovery & Breastfeeding/Pumping

By week 10 postpartum, your body has made significant healing progress. Most physical recovery from vaginal birth or C-section is well underway, though individual timelines vary. You may notice:

**Physical Recovery:**
- Uterus has returned to near pre-pregnancy size (though it may take longer if breastfeeding)
- Lochia should have stopped completely by now (if you're still experiencing bleeding or spotting, consult your provider)
- Perineal soreness or C-section incision pain should be significantly improved; any worsening pain, redness, or discharge needs evaluation
- Hormonal shifts continue to stabilize; you may still experience some night sweats as your body adjusts

**Breastfeeding & Pumping:**
If you're breastfeeding, your milk supply is likely well-established by week 10. You may notice:
- Breasts feel softer between feeds (this is normal - your body has adjusted to your baby's needs)
- Let-down reflex may feel less intense or happen more quickly
- Baby is likely feeding more efficiently, possibly in shorter sessions
- If pumping (for return to work or building a stash), aim to pump after morning feeds when supply is typically highest

> "constantly told my milk wasn't enough which honestly left me pretty scarred and anxious about supply" — Signal from r/breastfeeding

If you're feeling anxious about supply despite baby gaining weight well, remember: soft breasts don't mean low milk. Trust your baby's cues - adequate wet diapers (6+ per day) and steady weight gain are better indicators than breast fullness.

**Returning to Work Considerations:**
If you're planning to return to work soon:
- Start introducing a bottle 1-2 weeks before your return date (if baby hasn't taken one already)
- Practice your pumping schedule at home to build confidence
- Store milk properly: fresh milk can be refrigerated for up to 4 days or frozen for 6-12 months
- Consider discussing flexible scheduling or lactation accommodations with your employer

**Self-Care Tips:**
- Continue prenatal vitamins if breastfeeding
- Prioritize protein-rich foods and complex carbohydrates for sustained energy
- Gentle exercise like walking, postnatal yoga, or swimming is generally safe if cleared by your provider
- Pelvic floor exercises (Kegels) remain important for long-term bladder and bowel health

## Baby: Newborn Growth & Development

At 10 weeks old, your baby is becoming more interactive and developing new skills:

**Physical Development:**
- Weight gain continues steadily (average 1.5-2 lbs per month, but varies)
- Length increases about 1 inch per month
- Head circumference grows approximately 0.5 inches per month
- Many babies have outgrown newborn clothing and moved to 0-3 month sizes

**Motor Skills:**
- Baby can hold head up steadily when upright
- During tummy time, they may push up on forearms and hold chest off the ground
- Grasping reflex is becoming more voluntary - they may bat at toys or hold rattles briefly
- Legs kick strongly when lying on back; may begin to push down with feet when held upright

**Sensory & Social Development:**
- Vision is improving - baby can track objects smoothly and recognize familiar faces across the room
- Hearing is well-developed; they turn toward voices and may startle at loud noises
- Social smiles are consistent and frequent in response to your face and voice
- Cooing and vowel-like sounds ("ah-goo", "eee") emerge as early communication
- Baby enjoys looking at complex patterns, mirrors, and high-contrast images

**Feeding Patterns:**
- Breastfed babies typically feed 6-8 times in 24 hours (though some may still feed more frequently)
- Formula-fed babies usually take 4-5 oz per feeding, every 3-4 hours
- Growth spurts can still occur around this age, leading to temporary increases in feeding frequency
- Night sleep may be consolidating for some babies (4-6 hour stretches), though many still wake 1-2 times per night

**Sleep:**
- Total sleep: 14-16 hours per day (variable)
- Naps: 3-5 naps per day, ranging from 30 minutes to 2 hours
- Many babies experience a sleep progression around 3-4 months (sometimes called the "4-month sleep regression") where sleep patterns mature and they wake more frequently between sleep cycles

If your baby's sleep has suddenly worsened, know this is often a sign of developmental progress, not a setback. They're becoming more aware of their surroundings and cycling through sleep stages more like an adult.

## Mental Health: Navigating Ongoing Adjustment

By week 10, the initial "baby blues" have typically resolved, but new emotional challenges may emerge as you settle into your new normal:

**Common Experiences:**
- Fatigue from disrupted sleep continues to be a major factor in mood regulation
- Anxiety about returning to work (if applicable) or leaving baby with caregivers
- Body image concerns as pregnancy changes persist
- Feeling "touched out" from constant physical demands of breastfeeding/caring for baby
- Grief for your pre-baby life or identity, even while loving your new role
- Comparison traps (especially on social media) about baby's milestones or your "recovery"

> "don't want to mess this up" — Signal from Quora

If you're feeling overwhelmed, remember: this adjustment takes time. There's no timeline for "figuring it out." Small moments of self-care matter - even 5 minutes of deep breathing, stepping outside, or texting a friend can help reset your nervous system.

**When to Seek Additional Support:**
While some anxiety and mood fluctuations are normal, persistent symptoms may indicate postpartum depression or anxiety:
- Feeling sad, hopeless, or empty most days for more than 2 weeks
- Loss of interest in activities you usually enjoy
- Intense irritability or anger
- Anxiety that feels uncontrollable or includes panic attacks
- Intrusive thoughts about harm coming to baby (distressing but common; acting on them is rare and indicates urgent need for help)
- Thoughts of harming yourself or feeling that baby would be better off without you

If you experience any of these, please reach out to your provider, a therapist specializing in perinatal mental health, or call Postpartum Support International's helpline (1-800-944-4773). You are not alone, and help is available.

**Coping Strategies:**
- Connect with other parents - in-person groups or online communities can reduce isolation
- Accept that "good enough" parenting is truly enough for your baby's development
- Delegate tasks when possible - your time and energy are limited resources
- Practice self-compassion: speak to yourself as you would a good friend in your situation
- If returning to work, plan and practice your routine in advance to reduce morning stress

## Partner Playbook: How to Help in Week 10

Your partner's role evolves as baby grows and your needs shift. Here’s how they can support you this week:

**Feeding Support:**
- If you're pumping, partner can wash and sterilize pump parts, prepare bottles, and handle milk storage
- During night feeds, they can bring you water/snacks, change baby's diaper, and burp baby afterward so you can fall back asleep faster
- If supplementing with formula, they can prepare bottles and handle feeding sessions

**Sleep Support:**
- Take ownership of at least one night waking per night to allow you longer sleep stretches
- Handle early morning wakings so you can sleep in (even 30-60 minutes helps)
- Create a calming pre-sleep routine for baby (bath, story, lullaby) that they can lead
- If baby is experiencing sleep progression, partner can try different soothing techniques (rocking, white noise, pacifier) while you rest

**Household & Logistics:**
- Take the lead on meal planning and preparation - focus on nutritious, easy-to-reheat options
- Manage baby's laundry, bottle washing, and gear organization
- Handle grocery runs and errands, especially if you're exclusively breastfeeding and find it challenging to leave baby
- Coordinate childcare arrangements if returning to work (researching options, scheduling tours, handling paperwork)

**Emotional Support:**
- Check in specifically about how you're feeling physically and emotionally - not just "how are you?" but "What's been challenging today?"
- Validate your experiences without trying to fix them: "That sounds really hard" vs. "You should try..."
- Encourage and facilitate time for you to shower, nap, or pursue a hobby alone
- Notice and appreciate what you're doing - specific praise ("I saw how calmly you handled that feeding") means more than general thanks

**Baby Bonding:**
- Partner can take the lead on tummy time, reading books, or going for walks with baby in carrier/stroller
- They can establish their own soothing routines (infant massage, singing, dancing) that build their confidence and give you breaks
- Skin-to-skin time remains beneficial for both partner and baby - encourage chest-to-chest cuddles

## Call Doctor? Red-Flag Table for Week 10

|| Symptom | Normal | When to Call |
||---------|--------|--------------|
|| **Bleeding/Spotting** | Lochia should have stopped completely by week 10. Light spotting may occur occasionally in breastfeeding women due to hormonal fluctuations. | Heavy bleeding (soaking more than one pad per hour), bleeding that restarts after stopping, foul odor, fever/chills, or passing large clots. |
|| **Perineal/C-Section Pain** | Mild discomfort may persist, especially with prolonged sitting or activity. C-section numbness/itching around incision is common as nerves heal. | Increasing pain, redness, swelling, warmth, pus, or separation of incision/cut. Pain that interferes with daily activities or sitting/urination. |
|| **Breast Pain** | Occasional tenderness during let-down or if baby sleeps longer than usual (mild engorgement). Nipple sensitivity should be resolved if latch is good. | Red streaks on breast, fever/chills/flu-like symptoms (mastitis), cracked/bleeding nipples, persistent lump that doesn't drain after feeding, or severe pain with pumping/feeding. |
|| **Baby's Feeding** | Steady weight gain, 6+ wet diapers/day, content after feeds. Cluster feeding during growth spurts is normal. | Poor weight gain or weight loss, fewer than 6 wet diapers/day, projectile vomiting, green/bloody stools, lethargy, or refusal to feed for multiple sessions. |
|| **Baby's Sleep** | Varied patterns - some babies sleep longer stretches, others wake frequently. Sleep progressions around 3-4 months cause temporary worsening. | Extreme difficulty waking baby for feeds, baby seems overly lethargic or unresponsive, breathing pauses longer than 10 seconds, or persistent inconsolable crying for hours despite soothing attempts. |
|| **Emotional Health** | Mood fluctuations related to sleep loss, occasional anxiety about baby/parenting, moments of sadness or frustration that pass. | Persistent sadness/hopelessness >2 weeks, inability to enjoy baby, anxiety/panic attacks, intrusive thoughts of harm (to self or baby) that feel uncontrollable, or thoughts that you/your baby would be better off if you weren't here. |
|| **Fever** | None expected in parent. Low-grade fever (<100.4°F) can occur with mild engorgement or minor illness. | Temperature ≥100.4°F (38°C) in parent - could indicate infection (uterine, mastitis, UTI, etc.). In baby: rectal temperature ≥100.4°F (38°C) requires immediate pediatric evaluation regardless of other symptoms. |
|| **Urination/Bowel Movements** | Regular urination without pain/burning. Bowel movements vary - breastfeeding parents may have fewer BMs; formula-fed may be more regular. | Pain/burning with urination, inability to urinate, severe constipation with bleeding, bloody stools, or diarrhea lasting >24 hours. |
|| **Baby's Development** | Reaching milestones within typical ranges (with variation), making eye contact, responding to sounds, showing interest in surroundings. | Not responding to loud sounds, not tracking objects with eyes, not smiling socially by 3 months, persistent crossed eyes after 3 months, or lack of movement in arms/legs. |

**Remember**: When in doubt, call your provider. It's always better to check. Trust your instincts - you know your body and your baby best.
