# Week 1: Days 0-7 Postpartum

## Body: Maternal Recovery & Breastfeeding Initiation

In the first week after birth, your body is undergoing incredible changes as it begins to heal from pregnancy and delivery. You may experience vaginal soreness, uterine cramping (as your uterus shrinks back to its pre-pregnancy size), and lochia (postpartum bleeding) that starts heavy and red, gradually lightening over the days.

**Breastfeeding Initiation**: The first hour after birth is often called the "golden hour" for breastfeeding initiation. Skin-to-skin contact helps regulate your baby's temperature, heart rate, and breathing, and triggers your milk production. Aim to breastfeed within the first hour if possible, and then frequently thereafter—about 8 to 12 times in 24 hours. It's normal for your milk to take 2-5 days to "come in"; before that, you're producing colostrum, a nutrient-rich early milk that's perfect for your newborn's tiny stomach.

If you're feeling anxious about your milk supply, remember: colostrum is concentrated and your baby only needs teaspoons per feeding in the first few days. Frequent removal (via breastfeeding or hand expression) signals your body to produce more milk. By day 3-5, you should notice your breasts feeling fuller and hear your baby swallowing during feeds.

**Physical Recovery Tips**:
- Use ice packs or chilled witch hazel pads for perineal soreness.
- Take sitz baths (warm, shallow baths) to promote healing.
- Continue taking your prenatal vitamins, especially if breastfeeding.
- Stay hydrated and eat protein-rich foods to support tissue repair.
- Gentle walking as tolerated can boost circulation and mood.

## Baby: Newborn Care & Feeding

Your baby is adjusting to life outside the womb. In the first week, they will:
- Sleep 14-17 hours per day, but in short bursts (2-4 hours at a time).
- Need to feed every 2-3 hours (or 8-12 times per day) because their stomach is tiny.
- Have periods of alertness where they study your face and begin to recognize your voice.
- Exhibit reflexes like rooting (turning toward touch on their cheek) and sucking.

**Jaundice**: It's common for newborns to develop a yellow tint to their skin and eyes (jaundice) around day 2-4 as their liver matures and processes bilirubin. Mild jaundice often resolves on its own with frequent feeding, which helps bilirubin exit the body. However, if the yellowing spreads to the arms, legs, or abdomen, or if your baby is lethargic or feeding poorly, contact your pediatrician.

**Umbilical Cord Care**: Keep the stump clean and dry. Fold the diaper below it to avoid urine contact. It typically falls off within 1-2 weeks. A little bleeding or oozing when it separates is normal; signs of infection include redness, swelling, foul odor, or pus.

## Mental Health: The Emotional Rollercoaster

The first week postpartum is a whirlwind of emotions. You might feel elated, overwhelmed, tearful, anxious, or all of the above—sometimes within minutes. These mood swings, often called the "baby blues," are driven by rapid hormonal shifts (estrogen and progesterone plummet after birth) and are experienced by up to 80% of new mothers.

If you're feeling sad, irritable, or anxious, know that it's common and usually peaks around day 3-5 before resolving by week two. However, if these feelings persist beyond two weeks, feel severe, or include thoughts of harming yourself or your baby, it could be postpartum depression (PPD) or anxiety (PPA), which requires professional support.

**Coping Strategies**:
- Rest when the baby sleeps—even short naps help.
- Accept help from others with meals, chores, or holding the baby so you can shower or nap.
- Talk about your feelings with your partner, a friend, or a support group.
- Limit visitors if they're draining your energy.
- Stay hydrated and nourished; low blood sugar can worsen mood swings.

## Partner Playbook: How to Help in Week 1

Your partner plays a crucial role in your recovery and the baby's care. Here are concrete ways they can support you:

- **Feeding Support**: Bring you water and snacks during breastfeeding sessions, burp the baby after feeds, and change diapers so you can rest between feeds.
- **Household Relief**: Take charge of meals, laundry, and cleaning—especially in the first few days when you're limited to light activity.
- **Emotional Support**: Check in on how you're feeling, listen without trying to fix everything, and offer reassurance. Remind you that you're doing great.
- **Baby Bonding**: Encourage skin-to-skin time (baby on their bare chest) to help regulate the baby's temperature and strengthen their bond.
- **Night Shift**: Handle one night feeding (if bottle-feeding expressed milk) or bring the baby to you for breastfeeding and handle burping/diaper changes afterward so you can fall back asleep faster.

## Call Doctor? Red-Flag Table for Week 1

| Symptom | Normal | When to Call |
|---------|--------|--------------|
| **Bleeding (Lochia)** | Heavy red bleeding for first 3 days, then pink/brown, tapering off by day 7-10. | Soaking more than one pad per hour, large clots (golf ball size), foul odor, fever/chills. |
| **Perineal Pain** | Soreness, especially with stitches; improves each day. | Increasing pain, pus, red streaks, fever, or pain that interferes with sitting/urination. |
| **Breast Pain** | Engorgement (tight, full feeling) around day 3-5; sore nipples initially. | Cracked/bleeding nipples, flu-like symptoms (fever, chills, body aches), red streaks on breast (mastitis). |
| **Baby's Jaundice** | Yellowing of face and chest, peaks day 3-5, improves with feeding. | Yellowing reaches abdomen/arms/legs, baby is lethargic or feeding poorly, jaundice persists beyond week 2. |
| **Emotional Health** | Mood swings, tearfulness, irritability (baby blues), resolves by ~2 weeks. | Sadness/hopelessness lasting >2 weeks, inability to enjoy baby, anxiety/panic attacks, thoughts of self-harm or harming baby. |
| **Fever** | None expected. | Temperature ≥100.4°F (38°C) — could indicate infection (uterine, mastitis, UTI). |
| **Urination/Bowel Movements** | May be sore at first; urinating frequently helps reduce swelling. First BM may occur day 1-3. | Inability to urinate, severe constipation with pain/blood, or no BM by day 4. |

**Remember**: When in doubt, call your provider. It's always better to check.
