# Week 4: Days 22-28 Postpartum

## Body: Maternal Recovery & Breastfeeding and Pumping

By week four, your body continues to heal from pregnancy and delivery. Lochia (postpartum bleeding) should have transitioned to a lighter pink or brown discharge and may be tapering off. If you had a cesarean section, your incision is likely healing well, but avoid heavy lifting and strenuous activity until cleared by your provider.

**Breastfeeding and Pumping**: Around week four, breastfeeding is often more established, but challenges like supply concerns or pumping for returning to work may arise. Many mothers experience a growth spurt in their baby around this time, leading to increased feeding frequency (cluster feeding). This is normal and helps boost your milk supply. If you're pumping, aim to pump after breastfeeding sessions to build a freezer stash or maintain supply when separated from your baby. Remember: your milk supply is driven by demand—frequent milk removal signals your body to produce more.

**Physical Recovery Tips**:
- Continue gentle pelvic floor exercises (Kegels) as tolerated to support healing.
- Maintain good posture while breastfeeding or pumping to prevent back and neck strain.
- Stay hydrated and consume adequate calories (especially if breastfeeding) to support energy and milk production.
- Gradually increase activity with short walks; listen to your body and stop if you experience pain or increased bleeding.
- Schedule your postpartum checkup if you haven't already (typically around 4-6 weeks).

## Baby: Newborn Growth & Development

At four weeks old, your baby is becoming more alert and interactive. You may notice:
- Increased wakefulness during the day, with longer stretches of alertness.
- Beginning of social smiles (not just reflexive) in response to your voice or face.
- Improved head control during tummy time—may lift head briefly.
- Growth spurt signs: increased hunger, fussiness, and desire to feed more frequently (cluster feeding).

**Sleep Patterns**: Newborn sleep remains irregular, but you might start seeing slightly longer sleep stretches at night (though 2-4 hours is still common). Daytime naps are still short and frequent. Avoid sleep training at this age; focus on responsive feeding and comforting.

**Feeding**: Breastfed babies typically feed 8-12 times per day. Formula-fed babies may feed every 3-4 hours. Watch for hunger cues (rooting, sucking hands, fussiness) rather than sticking to a strict schedule. If you're concerned about milk supply, remember that frequent feeding during growth spurts is normal and temporary.

**Developmental Activities**: Engage your baby with eye contact, gentle talking, and tummy time (1-2 minutes, a few times daily) to strengthen neck and shoulder muscles. Avoid overstimulation—watch for signs like turning away or fussiness.

## Mental Health: Navigating the Fourth Trimester

By week four, the initial "baby blues" may have subsided for many, but it's important to monitor your emotional health. Feelings of overwhelm, anxiety, or sadness can persist or emerge as the reality of constant caregiving sets in. You might feel isolated or struggle with identity shifts.

If you're experiencing persistent sadness, irritability, anxiety, or feelings of hopelessness that interfere with daily life or bonding with your baby, consider reaching out for support. Postpartum depression (PPD) and anxiety (PPA) can onset anytime in the first year and are treatable with therapy, support groups, or medication.

**Coping Strategies**:
- Prioritize small moments of self-care: a shower, a cup of tea, or a few minutes of deep breathing.
- Connect with other mothers through virtual or in-person support groups (many pediatricians or hospitals offer postpartum groups).
- Share feeding or nighttime duties with your partner to allow for longer sleep blocks.
- Journal your thoughts or use a mood-tracking app to identify patterns.
- Accept help with meals, chores, or baby care—don't try to do everything alone.

## Partner Playbook: How to Help in Week 4

Your partner's support remains crucial as you navigate breastfeeding, sleep deprivation, and emotional adjustments. Here’s how they can help:

- **Feeding Support**: If you're pumping, your partner can handle bottle feeds (using expressed milk) so you can rest or pump. They can also wash pump parts and bottles, and store milk properly.
- **Household Relief**: Take over meal prep, grocery shopping, or cleaning to reduce your mental load. Consider preparing freezer meals for busy days.
- **Emotional Support**: Check in regularly—not just "How are you?" but "What do you need right now?" Listen without judgment and validate your feelings. Encourage you to take breaks.
- **Baby Bonding**: Encourage skin-to-skin time (baby on their bare chest) to calm the baby and strengthen their bond. They can also handle burping, diaper changes, or soothing techniques.
- **Night Shift**: Handle one night feeding (if bottle-feeding) or bring the baby to you for breastfeeding and manage burping/diaper changes afterward so you can fall back asleep faster.
- **Self-Care Advocacy**: Remind you to hydrate, eat, and rest. Offer to watch the baby while you nap, shower, or step outside for fresh air.

## Call Doctor? Red-Flag Table for Week 4

|| Symptom | Normal | When to Call |
|---------|--------|--------------|
| **Bleeding (Lochia)** | Light pink/brown discharge, tapering off; may be intermittent. | Heavy bleeding (soaking more than one pad per hour), large clots, foul odor, fever/chills, or sudden increase after lightening. |
| **Perineal/C-Section Pain** | Mild soreness or itching as stitches heal; gradual improvement. | Increasing pain, pus, redness, swelling, separation of incision, or fever. |
| **Breast Pain** | Occasional engorgement or tenderness; comfortable breastfeeding/pumping. | Cracked/bleeding nipples, flu-like symptoms (fever, chills, body aches), red streaks on breast (mastitis), or persistent lump. |
| **Baby's Weight Gain** | Regaining birth weight by day 10-14; steady gain thereafter (about 5-7 oz/week). | Not gaining weight or losing weight after two weeks, lethargy, poor feeding, or fewer than 6 wet diapers/day. |
| **Emotional Health** | Occasional overwhelm, mood swings, or anxiety; able to enjoy moments with baby. | Sadness/hopelessness lasting >2 weeks, inability to enjoy baby, anxiety/panic attacks, thoughts of self-harm or harming baby, or feeling detached. |
| **Fever** | None expected. | Temperature ≥100.4°F (38°C) — could indicate infection (uterine, mastitis, UTI, or incision). |
| **Urination/Bowel Movements** | Regular urination; bowel movements may vary (especially if breastfeeding). | Painful urination, inability to urinate, severe constipation with pain/blood, or no BM by day 4-5 postpartum (if not already resolved). |

**Remember**: When in doubt, call your provider. It's always better to check.
